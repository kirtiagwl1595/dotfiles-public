# Winston presentation framework

Source: Patrick Winston's MIT lecture "How to Speak", adapted from Alex Prompter's six-prompt thread (April 2026). Adapted here for business and customer decks.

## Before building, collect

1. Audience: who is in the room, what they already believe, what worries them.
2. Outcome: what the audience should do or decide afterward.
3. Time: minutes available, and who presents before and after.
4. The one idea they must remember.

If any are missing and cannot be inferred from the conversation, ask before drafting.

Build the deck as a file or a draft. Publishing it (as an artifact, a shared link or an upload through a connector) is outward-facing: ask first.

## 1. Open with an empowerment promise

- Slide 1 or 2 states what the audience will know or be able to do by the end. Specific: "By the end you will know X" rather than "Today we will talk about X".
- The first 60 seconds cover the promise, context, and why it matters now.
- No jokes, no "thank you for having me", no apologies in the opening. Put the opening script in the speaker notes.

## 2. Remove slide crimes

Audit every slide for:
- Too many slides for the time. Guide: about 1 slide per 1.5 to 2 minutes for customer talks.
- Too many words. Slides support the speaker; the speaker is the main event. Cut text until each slide reads in under 10 seconds.
- Small type. Winston's rule is 40pt. For dense technical or customer decks, adapt: headlines follow the brand template, body text 14pt minimum, nothing under 11pt except footers.
- Reading slides aloud. Speaker notes must add to the slide, never repeat it.
- No white space. Leave breathing room.
- Background clutter beyond what the brand template requires.
- Weak endings: "Thank you", "Questions?", or a collaborators list as the final slide.

Give a specific fix for every crime found, not only a flag.

## 3. Make the core idea stick (Winston's Star)

Define all five before drafting slides:
- **Symbol**: one visual that represents the idea. Reuse it across slides.
- **Slogan**: a short, repeatable phrase. It appears on the opening and closing slides.
- **Surprise**: the fact that challenges the audience's assumption.
- **Salient idea**: exactly one idea that stands above everything else.
- **Story**: how it works, why it matters, how we got here.

## 4. Structure to persuade

- Vision within the first few minutes: the problem the audience cares about and the approach.
- Proof of work: concrete steps and examples that show this has been done before.
- The close mirrors the opening: promise made, promise kept.
- Every slide advances either vision or proof. Cut anything that does neither.

## 5. Teach complex ideas with a demonstration

For the hardest concept, design a concrete demonstration or analogy: a physical prop, a live walkthrough, or a simple verbal image. Script where the audience should look and what they should notice.

## 6. Close with contributions

- Final slide is a contributions slide: what the audience now has or knows, tied back to the opening promise and slogan.
- It stays on screen through Q&A.
- Closing words salute the audience or state a clear call to action. Do not end on "thank you". Put the closing script in the speaker notes.

## Output checklist

- [ ] Promise slide or promise in the opening script
- [ ] Slogan on opening and closing slides
- [ ] Surprise stated explicitly on at least one slide
- [ ] Slide count fits the time
- [ ] Body text 14pt or larger; each slide readable in under 10 seconds
- [ ] Speaker notes include opening script, transitions, and closing script
- [ ] Final slide is contributions, not "Thank you" or "Questions?"
